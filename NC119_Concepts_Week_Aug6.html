Mark Burrows for NC House 119 · Concepts for the week of Aug 6

Six on-message concepts — pick two or three

Same visual system as the Public Records graphic. Every quote below is Mark's own words, sourced from his recorded footage or his own posts — nothing invented, nothing to walk back.

First — the hat question

You're not wrong that some of the hat shots are awkward, but I'd split it by what the hat is doing in the picture rather than banning it.

#13 — the hat works

Field shirt, real smile, woods behind him. He looks like a man who's been outside doing something. Use for recovery, canvassing, showing-up content.

#6 — no hat, authority

Blazer, level expression. Use for policy and money topics where he needs to read as steady and serious rather than friendly.

#11 — the wildcard

Him and the dog. Not a policy card — but dog photos reliably outperform everything else on Facebook. Worth one non-topic post.

The rule I'd use: hat when the content is about being out in the district, no hat when it's about competence in Raleigh. The hat only looks like costume when it's paired with a blazer (#3) — that's the combination to avoid entirely.

If you're only doing two

  • #1 Tax Shift — because it anchors the Aug 11 newsletter and it's the best line he's ever said on camera.
  • #5 Rebuilding — because Swain is the stated next focus and this is the only concept that speaks to it without contrast, without politics, and with his actual résumé as the argument.

If there's room for a third, #4 The Job is the cheapest win — it rides the momentum of the post that just worked, uses the same wardrobe, and takes the theme somewhere new rather than repeating it.

Two things to check before publishing

  • Concept 6 has a fact in it. The four-and-a-half-hour DMV wait was accurate when he recorded it. Confirm it still is, or cut the number and keep "half a day off work."
  • Every headline is two words for a reason. The reference graphic works because the display type is enormous. Three-word headlines will force ChatGPT to shrink it and the whole effect collapses. If you want a different topic, find the two-word version of it first.
